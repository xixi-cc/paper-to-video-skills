# Beamer content writing guide

Use the paper's argument, not a stock outline, to decide what the audience needs.

## Establish the argument

Before drafting, identify:

- the intended audience and what they should understand by the end;
- the precise question or gap;
- the studied system, assumptions, variables, and regime;
- the method, derivation, or inference chain;
- the strongest quantitative or qualitative evidence;
- the authors' interpretation and stated limitations.

Do not write from the abstract alone or fill inaccessible details with generic
scientific prose.

## Compose frames

Give each substantive frame one narrative job and one primary claim. Include the
definitions, steps, conditions, values, or comparisons needed to support that
claim, and make its connection to neighboring frames clear.

Prefer claim-led titles when the evidence supports them. Avoid repeated generic
titles such as “core mechanism”, “main results”, “physical meaning”, and “next
steps”. Neutral titles are appropriate for notation or a genuine open question.

Use complete, concise relationships rather than keyword lists:

- condition or parameter change → observed response → interpretation;
- method step → intermediate object → output;
- baseline difference → measured result → significance;
- assumption → equation or derivation → physical meaning.

Choose the number of frames from the requested duration and technical density.
Omit irrelevant sections. A paper without a central equation does not need an
equation page; a paper with few useful visuals does not need an arbitrary figure
quota.

## Evidence and figures

Preserve decisive values, parameter dependence, errors, uncertainty, and
comparison conditions. Every evidence item should change the interpretation,
test a prediction, distinguish explanations, or establish a conclusion boundary.

Keep a detailed paper figure on a dedicated frame. Retain readable axes, legends,
and panel labels. Its visible caption states what is varied or measured, the
comparison condition, the decisive trend or value, and why it matters.

For equations, preserve the paper's notation and assumptions. Define symbols and
state the equation's role and interpretation on the frame. Split only when the
derivation cannot remain legible.

Separate author-stated limitations from presentation inferences. State which
claim or extrapolation each limitation constrains.

## Cold read

After compilation, hide narration and read the complete deck in order. Verify
that the opening question, reasoning chain, central result, and conclusion
boundary are recoverable from visible content. Inspect every page at readable
size and revise the manifest directly.

Merge repeated or adjacent sparse frames when they form one argument. Split a
frame only when supported content is genuinely hard to scan. Never fill space
with invented details, generic background, repeated conclusions, or cosmetic
enlargement.

In a batch, apply this review independently to each paper. Share deterministic
infrastructure and caches, but not substantive frame bodies or conclusions.
